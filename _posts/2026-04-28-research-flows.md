*Stuff I've learned from doing research...*

Many Ph.D students, including myself, encounter a ton of frustration when doing research. School work is mostly about getting stuff done. But research doesn't work like that at all. You can't just power through things because ... it's often not clear what you should power through. 

I recently found some wisdom from Daoist philosophy that I think can be really helpful for navigating the research process. I'm copying all of this from this post [by Jason Bartlett](https://luminousdharma.org/return-to-simplicity-through-wuwei/) which is for more of a meditation audience. 

Most of us have developed habits to motor through life. But when we get in contact with the unknowns of research (and life), that approach leads to a ton of unnecessary friction and pain. As Jason writes: 

> Many of us have developed the tendency of leaning forward through life. By leaning forward, I mean our tendency to go through life pushing towards our goals. 
> 
> In this way of living, we regularly aim our efforts at where we think life should go next. The goal may be anything from a car, finding a partner, career changes etc.. Willfully trying to push forward, each goal is seen as the next step of life, with the time and effort it takes to reach each goal being perceived as at best, a formality, or as time we wish we didn’t have to waste.


There's so much wisdom here that is applicable to research. I would often feel like there were two distinct things when doing research: 

1. Reaching our goals (i.e. getting answers to reseach questions, getting our models to perform well)
2. All the obstacles and confusion we encounter when trying to reach (1)

And let's be honest... (2) encountering obstacles and confusion seems to comprise like >95% of time I have spent doing research.  

So if I view a majority of the time spent doing research is an obstacle (or worst, a waste of time), life as a Ph.D student will start to feel depressing. Jason discusses this as well:

> In this model, the reward is felt to be at the goal, and everything leading up to the goal tends to be seen as an inconvenient price that must be paid in order to try and reach the goal. Changing conditions are seen as obstacles and people and events that lead away from the goal are perceived as if they are enemies. We feel we must get past them to reach our goals.

Is it possible to re-orient in a way that makes life as a Ph.D student more fun and easeful?

## Research as Sailing

There are three concepts from Daoist philosophy that is helpful: 

- Wuwei (無為)
- Ziran (自然)
- Weiwuwei (為無為)

Wuwei might be thought of as absence of action. Ziran means nature. Weiwuwei is intuitive action (doing without motoring), or to act in harmony with nature.

Jason explains all this with a sailboat analogy,  which I am slightly adapting for our setting.  

> Picture that we are in a sailboat equipped with a motor. This boat is out on an open ocean and is currently motoring towards its destination. 

This is the beginning of our project. We might have some ideas of where we're headed and how long it takes. 

> If we turn off the motor this is wuwei. 
> 
> With the motor turned off we become aware of the natural existing conditions, like the strength and direction of the wind, the size of the waves, and the currents of the water. These natural conditions are ziran.

Once we've been motoring along for a bit, it's time to turn off the motor.  We can relax our assumptions about where this project is headed.  We can actually take in more data about what feedbacks we're getting from our research process.  We can become more receptive and stop forcing our assumptions about how the thing we're working on is supposed to work. 

> Putting up the sails, we feel and work with the existing conditions. This also includes observing how sailing is going and what destination makes sense considering the conditions that are present. This skillful sailing according to conditions is weiwuwei.

So once we stop obsessing about the goals, timelines, and whatever assumptions about the research project we've been holding, we can go from motoring through all this to sailing. The fuel is cleaner, we're more connected to what's around us, and we can enjoy the ride more (even if we don't know where we're headed exactly).
